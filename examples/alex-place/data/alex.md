## 2026-01-05 — Started the starter

Mixed flour and water in a jar tonight. I know it's just flour and water. I
feel a little silly being this excited about flour and water. We were doing
this one at school before everything stopped — bench fermentation, the smell
test, all of it. Bringing it back into the kitchen is going to be slow.

Dad would've made fun of me for the jar.

---

## 2026-01-20 — First real bake

Dense. Way too sour. I don't know what I expected from a first bake but I
thought it would at least *rise*. Lined up my suspects: cold retard probably
ran too long, the levain is very mature at this point (I've been refreshing
twice a day for two weeks), and the hydration was maybe too low for the
crumb I was going for. Could be any of them. Could be all three.

Not giving up. Square one isn't really square one — square one was October
and we are way past it.

---

## 2026-01-22 — Read the Field chapter on fermentation

Mara Field, *Sourdough Notes* — chapter on acidity. The point that landed:
**acidity is the sum of starter maturity, inoculation rate, total
fermentation time, and temperature schedule.** There is no single dial. I
have been treating cold retard like the one variable that mattered. It's one
of several.

OK. Shorter proof next time. Less mature levain. See what changes.

---

## 2026-02-08 — Room-temp proof bake

Different bread. Better bread. Open crumb, mild flavor, light tang —
nothing like that brick from January. I shortened the bulk, dropped the cold
retard entirely, and used a younger levain. Acidity dropped down to where I
wanted it. **At least with this starter** — I don't know yet if the
technique transfers.

Going to try whole wheat next, but that is a different conversation. For
now: this works.

If Dad could see this loaf he'd pretend he wasn't impressed and then eat
half of it.

---

## 2026-02-15 — Ate at Kintaro tonight

Bowl of miso ramen. Broth was almost milky — opaque, thick, coats the back
of the spoon. **Fat AND gelatin suspended through it, both — not just an
emulsified fat slick on top.** The vigorous boil pulled body out of the
bones. I could feel it in my mouth as much as taste it.

Trying this at home. Bones, water, time, and a real rolling boil. We'll see.
