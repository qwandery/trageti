# Browning Notes (fictional)

Maillard browning needs dry surface heat, enough time, and the right reactants: proteins and reducing sugars. A wet surface spends its heat evaporating water before browning can accelerate.

For roasted chicken skin, drying the surface and leaving it alone matters more than constant fussing. Movement interrupts contact, releases steam, and can keep the surface below the temperature range where browning compounds form quickly.
