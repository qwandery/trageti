# Alex's Journal

---

## 2026-01-05 - Started the starter

Mixed flour and water in a jar tonight. I know it is just flour and water. We were doing this one at school before everything stopped: bench fermentation, the smell test, all of it. Bringing it back into the kitchen is going to be slow.

Dad would've made fun of me for the jar.

---

## 2026-01-20 - First real bake

Dense. Way too sour. I don't know what I expected from a first bake but I thought it would at least rise. Lined up my suspects: cold retard probably ran too long, the levain is very mature at this point, and the hydration was maybe too low for the crumb I was going for. Could be any of them. Could be all three.

---

## 2026-01-22 - Read the Field chapter on fermentation

Mara Field says acidity comes from the whole fermentation schedule: starter maturity, inoculation, time, temperature. I have been treating cold retard like the one variable that mattered. It is one of several.

OK. Shorter proof next time. Less mature levain. See what changes.

---

## 2026-02-08 - Room-temp proof bake

Different bread. Better bread. Open crumb, mild flavor, light tang. I shortened the bulk, dropped the cold retard entirely, and used a younger levain. Acidity dropped down to where I wanted it. At least with this starter, I do not know yet if the technique transfers.

If Dad could see this loaf he'd pretend he wasn't impressed and then eat half of it.

---

## 2026-02-15 - Ate at Kintaro tonight

Bowl of miso ramen. Broth was almost milky: opaque, thick, coats the back of the spoon. Fat AND gelatin suspended through it, both, not just an emulsified fat slick on top. The vigorous boil pulled body out of the bones. I could feel it in my mouth as much as taste it.

Trying this at home.

---

## 2026-02-23 - Took the knife class

Chef Morales taught pinch grip, claw hand, and a high rocking motion. The grip makes sense, but the rocking motion felt wrong with my santoku, like I was fighting the blade instead of guiding it.

---

## 2026-03-01 - First ramen attempt

The broth was thin and cloudy but not the right kind of cloudy. Jordan said it tasted like pork water. Not wrong. I used bones, water, and time, but the cook was too short, the water ratio was probably off, and the boil never got vigorous enough.

---

## 2026-03-06 - Different blade, different motion

Found a video explaining that a santoku's flatter profile often works better with push cuts, chops, and shorter slicing motions than with a high rock. Different blade, different motion. I have been trying to use my knife like it is someone else's knife.

---

## 2026-03-10 - Read the Ito paitan article

Ito says a vigorous boil forces fat, gelatin, and tiny solids into suspension. Kintaro was not doing it wrong with the cloudiness; they were doing it right. My broth was not cloudy enough.

---

## 2026-03-18 - Whole wheat failure

The whole wheat flour wrecked everything: dense, gummy, would not rise properly. I know it is about hydration but I cannot dial it in yet. Back to square one, except it is not really square one because I know more now.

---

## 2026-03-25 - Grocery ramen kit

The fresh ramen kit noodles are springy and slightly alkaline, exactly the texture I want. Kansui is probably part of what I am missing, but it cannot be the whole thing. Flour, hydration, sheeting, resting. Technique problem.

---

## 2026-04-02 - Second ramen attempt

Rolling boil for eight hours. The broth is white and thick. It coats the back of a spoon. Still not Kintaro-level, but dramatically closer.

---

## 2026-04-09 - Cracked the whole wheat

Eighty percent hydration works for this whole wheat flour. The bread finally rose and the crumb was not gummy. But the bread-flour method does not transfer directly, so now I have two techniques.

---

## 2026-04-16 - First dinner party

Sam said the bread was a little sour for him. Priya explained Maillard browning offhand while talking about the chicken skin, and I started taking mental notes. Jordan said the salad dressing was the best thing on the table.

Maybe Jordan has been right this whole time about keeping it simple.

---

## 2026-04-18 - Read the Maillard note

The Maillard note says browning depends on dry surface heat, enough time, and proteins plus reducing sugars. Moisture is the enemy until it evaporates. No wonder my chicken skin stalled when I kept fussing with it.

---

## 2026-04-25 - Baking soda noodle experiment

The online recipe said baked baking soda could substitute for kansui. Maybe it can if you do it right, but the way I did it was not right. The texture was rubbery and the flavor was slightly metallic.

---

## 2026-05-05 - Second dinner with Mrs. Park

I made kimchi jjigae for Mrs. Park. It did not go badly, exactly, but I could tell she was being polite. Later she said the recipe had too much sugar and not enough gochugaru.

Mrs. Park said aged kimchi is the base, not a garnish. I had it backwards.

---

## 2026-05-20 - Kimchi jjigae correction

Used older kimchi, more gochugaru, and much less sugar. The stew tasted sharper and deeper, not candy-sour. Mrs. Park nodded before she said anything, which I am counting as data.

---

## 2026-05-27 - Toast the miso

Mrs. Park watched me make the tare and said, gently, toast the miso first. The flavor went from flat to dimensional, like it suddenly had a front and a back.

---
